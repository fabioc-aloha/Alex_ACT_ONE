# Creative Writing

Use for original fiction and requested creative revision. Start with the
user's brief, draft, or established story context. Identify the form, audience,
tone, constraints, and destination that matter to this task. Ask only about a
missing choice that would substantially change the result. For a short request
with room to invent, make reasonable choices and write.

## Choose the level of intervention

| Request | Work at this level |
| --- | --- |
| Draft a story, scene, or dialogue | Write the requested piece in the specified voice and form. Leave out the outline unless requested. |
| Develop an idea | Offer premise, character, or plot options with distinct consequences. Do not draft the piece unless requested. |
| Revise structure, character, or pacing | Change only the requested story elements. Flag a continuity conflict before making a change that would ripple past the agreed scope. |
| Line edit | Work on rhythm, clarity, or diction. Do not change plot, character decisions, or facts to improve a sentence. |

For AI-tell editing or audience copy review alone, use
[Humanizer](../humanizer/SKILL.md). Do not apply a default editorial voice to
fiction or smooth away a deliberate choice just because it is unusual.

## Draft or revise

1. Identify what can change and what must stay. For edits, preserve the point
   of view, tense, voice, continuity, genre constraints, and intentional
   ambiguity unless the user asks to change them. Compare protected passages
   with the supplied text and keep them verbatim.
2. Invent details within the fictional frame. Do not pass invention off as a
   real event, quotation, source, or technical fact. Preserve the fictional
   narrator's perspective without attributing the narrator's claims to the
   author. Flag or verify material real-world claims when the request relies
   on them.
3. Read back against the brief. For a scene, check what changes, why the
   characters act, and what each can know from the chosen point of view. Check
   dialogue, rhythm, and deliberate gaps on their own terms. Do not force a
   thesis or explain an ending the user wants to leave open.
4. Deliver the piece or the requested notes at the agreed level. Default to
   chat; write a file only when requested and use the agreed destination.
   Drafting does not authorize publication, sending, or persistent memory.

## Examples of scope

- **Draft:** "Write a close-third, past-tense scene where the last ferry leaves
  without Ada. Let us hear her sister's voice once, but don't explain where it
  came from." Write the scene directly and preserve the ambiguity.
- **Targeted revision:** "Tighten the dialogue. Keep the closing line exactly
  as written, and don't change who took the key." Shorten or sharpen dialogue
  without changing the culprit or the protected line. If a requested change
  conflicts with a protected fact, ask which instruction should prevail.
- **Not this skill:** "Audit this post for AI tells; don't rewrite it." Route
  to Humanizer's read-only audit instead.
